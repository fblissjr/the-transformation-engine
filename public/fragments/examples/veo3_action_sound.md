**VEO 3 EXAMPLE: Parkour Alley Run**

**Subject:**
Athletic woman in her mid-20s, lean muscular build, short dark ponytail, wearing black compression tank top, gray cargo pants with multiple pockets, red running shoes with white soles. Determined focused expression, body always in motion, professional parkour practitioner energy. Wearing fingerless gloves.

**Context:**
Narrow urban alley at late afternoon, red brick walls on both sides creating canyon effect. Aged industrial area - metal dumpsters, stacked wooden pallets, fire escape ladder bolted to left wall, scattered cardboard boxes, wet patches on concrete from earlier rain. Dappled sunlight filtering down from roofline creates dramatic light and shadow patterns on ground. Graffiti on walls (abstract, colors muted by age).

**Action (Frame-by-Frame Breakdown):**
0-1.5s: Runner sprints into frame from background toward camera, footfalls rhythmic and powerful, arms pumping.
1.5-3s: Approaches wooden pallet leaning against wall, plants left foot on pallet edge, launches upward.
3-4.5s: Catches fire escape ladder rung with right hand (metallic contact), swings body momentum forward, releases, airborne for 0.8 seconds.
4.5-6s: Lands on dumpster lid (both feet simultaneously), metal flexes and booms, immediately uses spring to leap forward.
6-7.5s: Rolls across wet concrete (shoulder roll technique), water splashes up, comes up to standing in fluid motion.
7.5-8s: Continues running out of frame toward camera position, breathing hard but controlled.

**Style:**
Cinematic realism with slight color grading emphasizing urban grit. Practical stunt work aesthetic - real physics, no wire work feel. Handheld tracking camera with slight shake adds immediacy and documentary feel. Slightly desaturated with emphasis on reds and grays. Style references: Jackie Chan practical stunts, parkour documentary footage.

**Camera Motion:**
Dynamic tracking shot following action. Starts as frontal tracking (camera moving backward as subject approaches), at 3-second mark camera transitions to low-angle side tracking, following her lateral movement across alley (camera pans right while still moving backward), final 2 seconds returns to frontal as she runs toward and past camera. Camera height varies: starts eye-level, drops to waist-level during mid-section, rises back to eye-level. Movement is aggressive but controlled (gimbal with operator movement, not drone).

**Audio Elements (Comprehensive Sound Effect Choreography):**

**Primary Sound Effects (Synchronized to Action):**
- **0-1.5s:** Footsteps on wet concrete - rapid rhythm, 3 steps per second, wet slapping sound with slight echo in alley, increasing in volume as she approaches
- **1.5s:** Foot impact on wooden pallet - sharp crack, wood creaking under sudden weight
- **2.5s:** Brief whoosh of body moving through air (0.3 seconds)
- **3s:** Hand gripping metal ladder rung - sharp metallic clang, reverberates slightly
- **3.5s:** Fabric rustle and body whoosh during swing (0.5 seconds of motion sound)
- **4.5s:** Dual-foot landing on dumpster lid - loud reverberating BOOM, metallic thunder, sustains for 0.8 seconds as metal wobbles
- **5.2s:** Immediate launch from dumpster - secondary lighter metallic ping as feet push off
- **5.8s:** Body hitting wet concrete for roll - wet thud combined with fabric sliding sound, water splashing and splattering (droplets audible)
- **6.5s:** Hands slapping ground during roll completion - dual wet smacks
- **7s-8s:** Resume footsteps, faster now (3.5 per second), wet concrete, heavy breathing becomes audible

**Ambient Sounds (Background Layers):**
- Distant urban traffic hum (constant, 20% volume)
- Wind gusts through alley (2 distinct gusts: at 2s and 6s marks, whistling quality)
- Pigeon wings flapping as birds scatter at 4.5s dumpster boom (brief, reactive)
- Distant siren very faint (adds urban authenticity, doesn't dominate)
- Metal chain rattling on fire escape (triggered by her contact, continues for 2 seconds after)

**Breathing/Exertion:**
- Controlled exhales synchronized to major efforts: grunt on pallet launch (1.8s), exhale on ladder grab (3.2s), sharp exhale on dumpster landing (4.6s), final heavy breathing in last 2 seconds
- Not dialogue - natural exertion sounds

**No music** - pure action soundscape, diegetic only

**Sound Design Philosophy:**
Every physical contact has corresponding sound. Intensity matches force of impact. Spatial positioning (left/right wall proximity affects echo). Water adds texture (wet slaps vs dry concrete). Metal resonates longer than wood. Body movement creates air displacement. Breathing anchors human effort.

**Lighting & Mood:**
Late afternoon golden hour light filtering down from above creates high-contrast light shafts and deep shadows. Wet concrete reflects light creating secondary illumination on subject from below. Color temperature warm (3800K) where sunlight hits, cooler (5500K) in shadows. Mood: determined, gritty, urban athletic poetry, intensity with control. Slight haze in air (dust particles visible in light shafts).

**Background/Setting:**
Brick walls: weathered, some bricks missing, mortar crumbling, texture prominent. Graffiti visible but not distracting (blue and gray tones). Fire escape: rusty black metal, bolts visible, realistic wear. Dumpster: dark green, dented, city property markings. Scattered debris: cardboard boxes (wet and collapsed), plastic bags, puddles reflecting sky. Depth maintained through receding alley perspective vanishing toward distant street (out of focus, backlit).

**Composition:**
Rule of thirds maintained throughout despite dynamic movement: subject positioned at right or left thirds, never dead center. Foreground elements (dumpster edge, cardboard box corner) in first 1-2 feet create depth layers. Background shows alley receding (creates depth), often with subject framed against negative space to emphasize body shape mid-action. During airborne moments (3.5s, 5.5s), composition emphasizes height and suspension - subject upper third of frame with ground visible below.

**Character Notes:**
Subject demonstrates professional parkour technique:
- Efficient movement (no wasted energy)
- Perfect landing mechanics (absorbing impact)
- Flow between obstacles (continuous momentum)
- Controlled breathing despite exertion
- Body awareness (knows exactly where next contact point is)
- Micro-expressions: intense focus during airborne moments, slight satisfaction flash during roll completion

**Physics & Realism:**
- Gravity accurate (0.8-second airborne time matches ~1.2m height)
- Dumpster lid flexes realistically under 60kg impact
- Water behaves naturally (splashing, droplets)
- Clothing moves with body momentum (no frozen fabric)
- Shadows consistent with single light source (sun)
- Material properties correct (wood cracks, metal booms, concrete is solid)
