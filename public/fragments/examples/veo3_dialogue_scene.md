**VEO 3 EXAMPLE: Job Interview Tension**

**Subject:**
Two characters in professional office setting. Woman (interviewer) in her late 40s, sharp features, silver-rimmed glasses, navy blazer, dark hair pulled back in tight bun, expression neutral but evaluating. Man (candidate) in his late 20s, nervous energy, charcoal suit slightly too large, fidgeting with resume folder, brown hair slightly disheveled, attempting composed expression but eyes betray anxiety.

**Context:**
Modern corporate office, glass-walled conference room. Behind interviewer: blurred cityscape through floor-to-ceiling windows, overcast day providing even lighting. Minimalist aesthetic - white table, two modern black chairs, no decorations. Clock visible on wall showing 2:47 PM. Single sheet of paper (his resume) on table between them.

**Action:**
Interviewer sits back in chair, arms crossed, studying candidate across table. She tilts head slightly, considering. Candidate shifts in seat, brings hand up to adjust collar, then returns hands to clasp on table (nervous control). Interviewer leans forward, picks up resume, glances at it, then makes direct eye contact. Candidate swallows visibly, sits straighter, attempting confidence.

**Style:**
Documentary realism, corporate aesthetic, naturalistic lighting and performance. Handheld camera feel (very subtle movement, mimics human observation). Color grading cool and professional - emphasizing blues and grays. Authentic awkward interview tension, not theatrical.

**Camera Motion:**
Slow dolly-in from medium two-shot showing both subjects at table (first 3 seconds), transitions to tighter framing favoring candidate's perspective (4-6 seconds), ends on close-up of interviewer's face as she speaks final line (7-8 seconds). Eye-level angle throughout. Movement is smooth but with slight human imperfection (0.4 meters forward over 8 seconds).

**Audio Elements (Quotation Mark Formatting):**

**Dialogue (Primary Audio):**
- Interviewer's voice, professional and measured with slight skepticism: "Your experience is... limited." (spoken at 2-3 second mark)
- Brief pause (ambient office sounds only, 1 second)
- Candidate's voice, controlled but with nervous edge, trying to sound confident: "I'm a fast learner. Give me two weeks." (spoken at 5-7 second mark)

**Ambient Sounds (Background Layers):**
- Air conditioning hum (constant, low)
- Muffled office sounds through glass walls (distant phones, printer, voices - all indistinct)
- Paper rustle as interviewer picks up resume (3-second mark, synchronized to action)
- Clock ticking subtly audible (adds tension)
- Candidate's slight inhale before speaking (4.8-second mark)

**No music** - naturalistic office scene, diegetic sounds only.

**Lighting & Mood:**
Cool diffused natural light from overcast sky through windows (soft, even, corporate). Subtle overhead fluorescent fill creates slight catchlight in glasses. Color temperature around 5000K (neutral, slightly clinical). Mood: professional tension, power dynamic evident, uncomfortable evaluation moment. Not harsh, but not warm - businesslike distance maintained.

**Background/Setting:**
Through glass wall behind candidate: out-of-focus office activity (person walking past, others at desks working, generic corporate environment). Cityscape visible through windows behind interviewer (tall buildings, gray sky, creating depth). Office plants barely visible in bokeh. Minimal distractions - focus stays on character interaction.

**Composition:**
Initial two-shot follows rule of thirds: interviewer right third, candidate left third, negative space between emphasizes distance and power dynamic. Resume on table creates foreground interest (shallow focus). As camera dollies in, composition tightens to more confrontational framing. Final close-up on interviewer positioned at upper right intersection point, looking frame left toward candidate (who is now off-screen), implies her perspective and power.

**Character Notes:**
Interviewer: Body language is controlled power - crossed arms signal closed-off evaluation, forward lean shows engagement but also intimidation. Micro-expressions: slight eyebrow raise at "limited", almost imperceptible head nod at "fast learner" (acknowledging but not committing).

Candidate: Body language reveals anxiety despite verbal confidence - fidgeting, collar adjustment, swallowing, then forced stillness. Voice control attempts to mask nervousness. His "two weeks" statement is his shot at turning situation around - desperation masked as boldness.

**Dialogue Timing Breakdown:**
- 0-2s: Silent tension, establishing shot
- 2-3s: Interviewer speaks (5 words - well within optimal range)
- 3-4s: Pause (candidate processes, tension builds)
- 4-5s: Candidate prepares response (visible inhale)
- 5-7s: Candidate speaks (10 words - optimal length)
- 7-8s: Interviewer's reaction shot (no dialogue, expression tells story)

**Why This Works:**
- Short dialogue phrases (5-10 words each) optimal for lip sync
- Pauses between speakers allow audio breathing room
- Camera movement toward candidate during his line increases his moment of vulnerability
- Final reaction shot has no dialogue - circumvents lip sync entirely while maintaining narrative
- Power dynamic clear through blocking, camera angle, dialogue pacing
- Authentic workplace tension captured in 8 seconds

**Demonstrates:**
- Proper quotation mark formatting for dialogue
- Voice characteristic specification (professional/measured vs controlled/nervous)
- Strategic use of pauses and reaction shots
- Layered ambient soundscape (4 distinct environmental sounds)
- Micro-narrative: challenge presented → response attempted → outcome uncertain
- Two-character blocking and chemistry
- Documentary realism style with naturalistic audio
