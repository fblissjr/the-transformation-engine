# Dialogue & Sound Effects Scene Template (Veo 3.1)

## Scene Type: Dialogue & Sound Effects

### Characteristics
- **Framing**: Close-up to medium shots (character-focused)
- **Camera**: Intimate, minimal movement (unless motivated by action)
- **Audio Priority**: Dialogue clarity, explicit SFX, layered ambiance
- **Word Count**: 200-300 words
- **Best For**: Character interactions, conversations, dramatic reveals, tense exchanges

## Optimal Structure

```
[Shot type + framing] of [subjects] [action/position], [environment details].

[Character 1] ([body language/action]): [Delivery style] "[Quoted dialogue]"

[Character 2] ([body language/action]): [Delivery style] "[Quoted dialogue]"

[Optional: Additional dialogue exchange]

[Sound effects layer 1: Primary sounds], [Sound effects layer 2: Secondary sounds], [Sound effects layer 3: Ambient sounds]
```

## Audio Design (Critical)

### Dialogue Guidelines
- **Always use quotes** for all speech
- **Specify delivery style**:
  - Emotional: murmurs, whispers, shouts, excitedly, nervously, confidently
  - Physical: voice tight with fear, breathlessly, through gritted teeth
  - Temporal: slowly, urgently, hesitantly, matter-of-factly

### Character Actions During Dialogue
- Body language: leans forward, steps back, crosses arms
- Facial expressions: eyes narrow, face pales, jaw clenches
- Physical actions: hand on weapon, fingers tapping table, reaches for object

### Sound Effects Hierarchy
1. **Primary SFX**: Directly related to action (footsteps, door closes, glass breaks)
2. **Secondary SFX**: Environmental interactions (wind through broken window, water dripping, fabric rustling)
3. **Ambient Audio**: Constant environmental soundscape (distant traffic, building hum, nature sounds)

## Shot Composition Preferences

### Recommended Shots for Dialogue
- **Two-shot**: Both characters visible, showing dynamic
- **Close-up**: Single character, emphasizes emotion
- **Over-shoulder**: Intimate conversation framing
- **Medium close-up**: Character + immediate environment
- **Rack focus**: Shift attention between characters

### Camera Movement
- **Minimal by default**: Let performance carry scene
- **Slow push-in**: Build intensity
- **Static**: Observational, documentary feel
- **Slight handheld**: Tension, realism

## Example 1: Tense Investigation

```
Close-up of two detectives in a dimly lit warehouse, flashlight beams cutting through dusty air. They stand over an open crate, staring down at its contents, faces illuminated from below.

Detective Harris (veteran, hand instinctively moving to holster): Gravely, "I haven't seen one of these since the Peterson case. That was twenty years ago."

Detective Chen (rookie, voice tight with tension, taking half-step back): "Wait... Peterson? The file that's still sealed?" Eyes darting between Harris and the crate.

Harris (jaw clenched, not looking away from crate): Quietly, almost to himself, "Sealed for a reason. This shouldn't exist anymore."

Flashlight beams flickering slightly from unsteady hands, distant sound of rain on metal roof, Chen's breathing quickens, faint echo of warehouse ambiance, water dripping somewhere in darkness.
```

**Analysis**:
- Two exchanges (4 dialogue beats across 8 seconds)
- Clear delivery cues (gravely, tight with tension, quietly)
- Body language integrated (hand to holster, step back, jaw clenched)
- Layered audio (flashlights, rain, breathing, dripping, ambiance)
- 180 words (within 200-300 target)

## Example 2: Urgent Discovery

```
Wide shot transitioning to close-up of two hackers in a server room, multiple monitors casting blue glow on their faces, alarm flashing red on main screen. They're frozen, staring at scrolling code.

Alex (senior, leaning forward intensely, fingers still on keyboard): "Look at this. Someone's been inside our network for weeks." Voice low but urgent.

Jordan (junior, eyes widening, voice rising with each word): "Wait... that signature. That's OUR encryption. How is that—" (cuts self off, hand covering mouth)

Alex (turning slowly to face Jordan, expression darkening): Through gritted teeth, "Either someone cloned our keys, or..."

Jordan (finishing the thought, barely above whisper): "...or it's an inside job."

Rapid keyboard typing suddenly stops creating silence, alarm beeping intensifies (urgent pattern), server fans humming loudly, chair squeaks as Jordan leans back in shock, distant footsteps in hallway outside, low ambient hum of cooling systems.
```

**Analysis**:
- Multiple short exchanges (high energy)
- Interruption technique (dialogue cuts off)
- Shared realization (completes each other's thoughts)
- Audio shifts with action (typing stops = dramatic silence)
- Specific alarm pattern described
- 165 words (concise, tension-focused)

## Example 3: Quiet Revelation

```
Medium shot of therapist and client in softly lit office, afternoon sunlight filtering through sheer curtains. They sit in armchairs facing each other, coffee table between them with tissue box. Client grips armrest tightly.

Dr. Morrison (calmly, with gentle authority): "You've been avoiding this memory for months. What happens when you try to remember that day?"

Sarah (client, staring at hands, voice barely audible): "I... I see the door. The blue paint was chipped near the handle. I remember that so clearly."

Morrison (leaning forward slightly, maintaining eye contact): Softly encouraging, "And when you reached for that door handle?"

Sarah (long pause, breath catches, eyes glistening): Whispering, breaking slightly, "I knew. Before I even opened it, I already knew."

Quiet clock ticking on wall, soft fabric sound as Sarah shifts in chair, distant muffled traffic from street below, gentle HVAC whisper, slight rustle of Morrison's notepad, Sarah's breathing becoming measured and controlled.
```

**Analysis**:
- Slower pacing (therapeutic setting)
- Pauses indicated (long pause, breath catches)
- Quiet audio palette (ticking, soft sounds)
- Emotional builds through delivery (barely audible → whispering, breaking)
- Minimal but purposeful camera movement
- 162 words (efficient, emotion-focused)

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Dialogue Mistakes
- ❌ No quotes around speech
- ❌ Generic delivery ("says", "speaks")
- ❌ No character body language
- ❌ Dialogue too long for 8 seconds
- ✅ Quoted speech with specific delivery and physical action

### Audio Mistakes
- ❌ Vague sounds ("noises in background")
- ❌ Missing ambient layer
- ❌ Audio not tied to visual action
- ❌ No audio perspective (near/far)
- ✅ Explicit, layered, spatially aware audio design

### Framing Mistakes
- ❌ Too wide (loses facial detail)
- ❌ Camera movement distracts from dialogue
- ❌ No shot size specified
- ✅ Close enough to see expressions, static or motivated movement

## Checklist for Dialogue Scenes

- [ ] All dialogue in quotes
- [ ] Delivery style specified for each line
- [ ] Character actions/body language included
- [ ] Shot size/framing described (close-up, medium, two-shot)
- [ ] Three layers of audio (primary SFX, secondary SFX, ambient)
- [ ] Lighting atmosphere described
- [ ] Word count 200-300
- [ ] Camera movement minimal or motivated
- [ ] Emotional arc clear through delivery progression
- [ ] Environment supports mood

## When to Use This Template

### Perfect For:
- Interrogation scenes
- Tense discoveries
- Emotional conversations
- Confrontations
- Partner dynamics
- Therapy/counseling
- Negotiations
- Revelations

### Not Ideal For:
- Action sequences (use cinematic template)
- Establishing shots (use cinematic template)
- Abstract concepts (use animation template)
- Monologues without interaction
